# Decision: v1.29 freeze verdict (owner, 2026-10-05)

## Why
The 2026-09-21 decision (`2026-09-21-v1.29-use-it-every-day.md`) ended v1.29 with a two-week
freeze: the owner uses Aether for real work in his own projects, only what he actually hits gets
fixed, and a stopping rule agreed up front decides whether investment in the full framework
continues. The freeze ran from 25 September 2026. The owner closed it four days early, on
5 October, choosing "Close the trial now" over waiting for 9 October.

## The count
**19 blockers**, read from `.planning/phases/210-two-week-freeze/210-BLOCKERS.md` under the
definition fixed before the freeze: something that made the owner stop his real work and come to
Aether's chat to report it or get it fixed. Every row is in
`.planning/phases/210-two-week-freeze/210-FREEZE-REPORT.md`. 18 are fixed; row 9 was left by the
owner's choice. The owner does not know whether the log is complete ("Don't know", 2026-10-05;
"i dunno", 2026-09-28).

## The owner's per-project answers (2026-10-05)
Asked, for each project, whether he took one real piece of work from start to finish through
Aether without coming to this chat because Aether went wrong:
- **French Fluency:** "No".
- **Finish the Track deck** (Eno inspiration and creative flashcards): "No".
- **Pocket-Chopper:** "Don't count it" — GSD work, not an Aether project; its one stop stays in
  the count.

## The verdict: an override, not the rule met
The stopping rule says that more than about one blocker a week (about two over the freeze) means
investment in the full framework stops. The count is 19. The owner chose **"Keep going"**.
Told that, against this count, it is recorded as overruling the rule, he chose this reason, in
his own words from 3 October:

> "I just want to have it that it doesn't have any of these fucking issues again"

meaning: fix what he hits rather than drop the framework. By 5 October every recorded stop except
row 9 (left by his choice) had a fix installed.

The rule was **not met**. Investment continues by the owner's explicit override, with the count
unchanged and the definition unchanged.
