<objective>
Close the two-week trial honestly: report the real count, get the owner's own verdict on
whether the framework earned its place, and write down what happens next.

Purpose: the fortnight only means something if its answer is stated plainly, including when
that answer is that investment in the full framework stops. This plan exists so that outcome
is reachable rather than quietly avoided.

Output: one report holding the count and the facts, the owner's verdict recorded as a dated
decision, two requirements settled on real evidence, and the temporary recorder taken back out
of CLAUDE.md.
</objective>

<the_question_being_answered>
From `.planning/decisions/2026-09-21-v1.29-use-it-every-day.md`, agreed before the fortnight
rather than after it:

> If, after the freeze, he still hits a blocker more than about once a week, investment in the
> full framework stops. The parts that already earn their place (quick jobs, flags, the memory
> of his preferences and lessons) are kept on top of plain Claude.

Over a fortnight, "about once a week" is roughly two. So the number that matters is the count
of rows in `.planning/phases/210-two-week-freeze/210-BLOCKERS.md`, under the definition fixed
in `210-FREEZE-START.md` before any of those rows existed: something that made the owner stop
his real work and come to Aether's chat to report it or get it fixed.

Two ways this plan can fail its own purpose. Both are named here so they are recognised if they
start happening:

- **Re-cutting the definition once the numbers are in.** Deciding after the fact that some rows
  "were not really blockers" is how a failing result gets turned into a passing one. The
  definition is fixed; the count is the count.
- **Reporting the count but never stating the verdict.** A report that lists what happened and
  stops short of saying what it means has not done this phase's job.
</the_question_being_answered>
