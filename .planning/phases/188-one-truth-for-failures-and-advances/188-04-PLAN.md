<objective>
Turn ROADMAP criterion 4 ("grep-ratchet against non-atomic COLONY_STATE writes") into a genuine
structural property check, following the exact model the planning brief named:
`cmd/worktree_destruction_reachability_test.go` parses the AST, builds a call graph, discovers
sites structurally, and fails loudly rather than vacuously when it finds nothing.

This is deliberately **not** a blanket "every non-atomic COLONY_STATE.json write must become
atomic" migration — a repo-wide grep found ~30 such call sites across ~25 files, most of them
one-shot CLI commands (`init`, `colonize`, `abandon`, `entomb`, and similar) with no realistic
concurrent writer. Converting all of them is the ROADMAP's own deferred `LOCK` requirement
category, not this phase's goal, and would exceed a single plan's safe context budget by a wide
margin (see 188-CONTEXT.md decision D-07 for the full reasoning).

Instead, this plan builds two structural checks: a shrink-only baseline allowlist (so nothing
*new* can regress unnoticed, mirroring `cmd/subcommand_reachability_ratchet_test.go`'s proven
`-update-orphan-allowlist` idiom), and a zero-tolerance check that nothing reachable from
188-02's new `advancePhase()` function is ever allowed a non-atomic write, no allowlist exemption
possible — because that one function is the actual subject of "one phase-advance discipline."

Purpose: ROADMAP criterion 4, scoped per D-07.

Output: `cmd/colony_state_atomicity_ratchet_test.go` (new), `cmd/testdata/colony_state_write_allowlist.json`
(new, the checked-in baseline).

function, record the enclosing function name as empty and let that surface as a normal allowlist
entry rather than crashing).
